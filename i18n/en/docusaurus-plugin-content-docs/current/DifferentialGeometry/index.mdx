# Differential Geometry and Fibre Bundle for Babies

## Preface

It is well known that textbooks, notes, and lecture materials in mathematics and physics have a long-standing tradition of deliberately presenting content in an obscure and difficult-to-understand manner. This practice—erasing the traces of thought processes while leaving only elegant results—has created a deep and formidable barrier between beginners and mathematical knowledge. This became especially evident to me when I attempted to self-study differential geometry, general relativity, fibre bundles, and gauge theory.

With this mindset, I began writing these notes, attempting to rephrase the abstract and concise formal language of differential geometry and fibre bundles in a more fundamental and accessible way, aiming to reconstruct the clean traces of previous thinkers' insights and to offer some intuitive guidance for concepts that are often difficult to grasp.

> During the learning process, I realized that I was gradually losing the ability to express certain ideas in plain language—perhaps this is a kind of intellectual contamination. Maybe the textbooks are not deliberately obscure.

When writing these notes I referred mainly to the following three sources:

- Liang Canbin and Zhou Bin, *Differential Geometry and General Relativity*;
- [An Introduction to Differential Geometry (for Physicists)](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/629852598);
- [Fibre bundles, connections and the Berry phase](https://switefaster.github.io/blog/fibre-bundle/).
