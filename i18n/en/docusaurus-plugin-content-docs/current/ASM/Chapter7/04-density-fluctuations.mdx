# 7.4 Density Fluctuation in Grand Canonical Ensemble

In this section, we compute density fluctuations within the grand canonical ensemble, which is given by

<Anchor id="eq-7-39" />
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle N^2\rangle - \langle N\rangle^2 &= \frac{k^2T^2}{\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T)}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\mu^2}\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T) - \left[\frac{kT}{\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T)\right]^2\\
&= k^2T^2\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\mu^2}\ln\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T) = \frac{1}{\beta^2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\mu^2}\left(\frac{PV}{kT}\right) = kTV\frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial\mu^2},
\end{aligned} \tag{7.39}
$$

where we use the following identity

$$
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}\ln\mathcal{Q} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{Q}}{\partial x}\right) = \frac{1}{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{\partial^2\mathcal{Q}}{\partial x^2} - \left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{Q}}{\partial x}\right)^2.
$$

Next, we shall cast the density fluctuation (more precisely, the second derivative $\partial^2 P/\partial\mu^2$ appearing on the right-hand side) into a more familiar and physically transparent form. As an extensive quantity, the Helmholtz free energy $A(N, V, T)$ satisfies the scaling relation:

$$
A(\lambda N,\lambda V,T) = \lambda A(N,V,T).
$$

Thus, we may express it as:

<Anchor id="eq-7-40" />
$$
A(N,V) = Na(v), \tag{7.40}
$$

where $v = V/N$ is the volume per particle, and $a(v)$ is the Helmholtz free energy per particle. For simplicity, we suppress the temperature $T$ (a fixed external parameter) in the notation. The chemical potential $\mu$ and pressure $P$ (both intensive) are then given by:

<Anchor id="eq-7-41" />
$$
\mu = \left\{\frac{\partial[Na(v)]}{\partial N}\right\}_V = a(v) + N\frac{\partial a}{\partial v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial N} = a(v) - v\frac{\partial a(v)}{\partial v} \tag{7.41}
$$

<Anchor id="eq-7-42" />
$$
P = -\left\{\frac{\partial[Na(v)]}{\partial V}\right\}_N = -N\frac{\partial a(v)}{\partial v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial V} = -\frac{\partial a(v)}{\partial v}. \tag{7.42}
$$

Consequently, the second order derivative $\partial^2 P/\partial\mu^2$ can be calculated as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial\mu}\right)_V &= -\frac{\partial^2 a}{\partial v^2}\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial\mu}\right)_V = -\frac{\partial^2 a}{\partial v^2}\left(\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial v}\right)_V^{-1} = -\frac{\partial^2 a}{\partial v^2}\left(-v\frac{\partial^2 a}{\partial v^2}\right)^{-1} = \frac{1}{v},\\
\left(\frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial\mu^2}\right)_V &= -\frac{1}{v^2}\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial\mu}\right)_V = \frac{1}{v^2}\left(v\frac{\partial^2 a}{\partial v^2}\right)^{-1} = \frac{1}{v^3}\left(\frac{\partial^2 a}{\partial v^2}\right)^{-1} = -\frac{1}{v^3\partial P/\partial v}.
\end{aligned}
$$

We emphasize again that the temperature $T$ is kept invariant in above derivations. Recall the definition of the isothermal compressibility $\kappa_T$

<Anchor id="eq-7-43" />
$$
\kappa_T \equiv -\frac{1}{v}\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial P}\right)_T = -\frac{1}{v(\partial P/\partial v)}. \tag{7.43}
$$

Using this result, we can finally write the fluctuation of the particle density as

<Anchor id="eq-7-44" />
$$
\langle N^2\rangle - \langle N\rangle^2 = kTV\frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial\mu^2} = kTV\frac{1}{v^2}\kappa_T = \langle N\rangle kT\kappa_T/v. \tag{7.44}
$$

For most physical systems, the isothermal compressibility $\kappa_T$ is positive and finite. In such cases, the relative fluctuation in the particle density is negligible. This is no longer true when $\kappa_T$ diverges. Such a divergence can occur at a first-order phase transition, where $(\partial P/\partial v)_T = 0$, and also at the critical point of a continuous phase transition.

Eq. [(7.44)](#eq-7-44) is analogous to Eq. [(7.18)](./02-energy-fluctuations.mdx#eq-7-18) and provides another example of the *fluctuation-dissipation theorem*, which states that the fluctuations of a physical quantity are generally related to a corresponding *generalized susceptibility*. In Eq. [(7.18)](./02-energy-fluctuations.mdx#eq-7-18), the energy fluctuation is related to the heat capacity at constant volume, $C_V$; in Eq. [(7.44)](#eq-7-44), the density fluctuation is related to the isothermal compressibility, $\kappa_T$.

The probability for a system to contain $N$ particles in the grand canonical ensemble is proportional to:

<Anchor id="eq-7-45" />
$$
W(N) = z^N Q_N(V,T) = e^{\beta[\mu N - A(N,V,T)]}, \tag{7.45}
$$

where $A(N,V,T)$ is the Helmholtz free energy for the canonical ensemble with $N$ particles. When density fluctuations are negligible (away from phase transitions), $W(N)$ is sharply peaked at $N = \langle N\rangle$, with a width of order $\sqrt{\langle N\rangle}$. Thus, the most probable particle number is simply the mean particle number, $\bar{N} = \langle N\rangle$. Furthermore,

$$
\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T) = \sum_N W(N) \approx W(\bar{N}).
$$

From Eq. [(7.45)](#eq-7-45), the Helmholtz free energy is determined by its value at the most probable particle number, $N = \bar{N}$:

<Anchor id="eq-7-46" />
$$
A(\bar{N},V,T) \approx \mu\bar{N} - kT\ln W(\bar{N}) = kT\left[\bar{N}\ln z - \ln\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T)\right]. \tag{7.46}
$$

This result agrees with the Euler relation $A = U - TS = -PV + \mu N$.

**Remark:** During a phase transition, the isothermal compressibility $\kappa_T$ diverges, and density fluctuations become significant (as seen in critical opalescence). Nevertheless, Eq. [(7.46)](#eq-7-46) remains valid, as will be shown later in this chapter.
