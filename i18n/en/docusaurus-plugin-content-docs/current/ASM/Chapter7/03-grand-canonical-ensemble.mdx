# 7.3 Grand Canonical Ensemble

In physics, the canonical ensemble, which allows only energy exchange, does not cover all situations. Many systems also exchange particles with their surroundings, which calls for a more general framework: *the grand canonical ensemble*.

The phase space of the grand canonical ensemble is the union of the phase spaces for systems with particle numbers $N = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$. Unlike the canonical ensemble, each representative point now carries an additional label $N$, written as $(q,p,N)$. To derive the distribution $\rho(q,p,N)$, we consider a small (but still macroscopic) subsystem of volume $V_1$ embedded in a larger system of volume $V$ and temperature $T$ described by a canonical ensemble. The remainder, of volume $V_2 = V - V_1$, acts as a particle reservoir. Let $N_1$ be the number of particles in the subsystem and $N_2 = N - N_1$ the number in the reservoir, with both $N_1$ and $N_2$ being random variables.

By construction, the system volume $V_1$ is fixed and much smaller than the reservoir volume $V_2$. Consequently, the most probable value of $N_1$ is significantly smaller than that of $N_2$ in equilibrium, though $N_1$ may fluctuate over the entire range from 0 to $N$. Thus, we have the inequalities:

$$
N_1 \ll N_2 \approx N,\quad V_1 \ll V_2 \approx V.
$$

The total Hamiltonian decomposes as: $\mathscr{H}(q,p) = \mathscr{H}_1(q_1,p_1)+\mathscr{H}_2(q_2,p_2)$, where subscripts 1 and 2 refer to the system and reservoir, respectively. Boundary effects between the system and reservoir are neglected.

As we have derived in the previous sections, the partition function of the entire system can be written as

<Anchor id="eq-7-26" />
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_N(V,T) &= \frac{1}{N!h^{3N}}\int d^{3N}q\,d^{3N}p\,e^{-\beta\mathscr{H}(q,p)}\\
&= \frac{1}{N!h^{3N}}\sum_{N_1=0}^{N}\frac{N!}{N_1!N_2!}\int_{V_1} d^{3N_1}q_1\,d^{3N_1}p_1\int_{V_2} d^{3N_2}q_2\,d^{3N_2}p_2\,e^{-\beta\mathscr{H}(q,p)}\\
&= \sum_{N_1=0}^{N}\int_{V_1}\frac{d^{3N_1}q_1\,d^{3N_1}p_1}{N_1!h^{3N_1}}e^{-\beta\mathscr{H}_1(q_1,p_1)}\int_{V_2}\frac{d^{3N_2}q_2\,d^{3N_2}p_2}{N_2!h^{3N_2}}e^{-\beta\mathscr{H}_2(q_2,p_2)}\\
&= \sum_{N_1=0}^{N} Q_{N_2}(V_2,T)\int_{V_1}\frac{d^{3N_1}q_1\,d^{3N_1}p_1}{N_1!h^{3N_1}}e^{-\beta\mathscr{H}_1(q_1,p_1)}.
\end{aligned} \tag{7.26}
$$

The probability density of the system $\rho$ can be defined as follows

<Anchor id="eq-7-27" />
$$
\rho(q_1,p_1,N_1) = \frac{Q_{N_2}(V_2,T)}{Q_N(V,T)}\,e^{-\beta\mathscr{H}_1(q_1,p_1)} \tag{7.27}
$$

which is normalized according to Eq. [(7.26)](#eq-7-26),

<Anchor id="eq-7-28" />
$$
\sum_{N_1=0}^{N}\int_{V_1}\frac{d^{3N_1}q_1\,d^{3N_1}p_1}{N_1!h^{3N_1}}\,\rho(q_1,p_1,N_1) = 1. \tag{7.28}
$$

Using Eq. [(7.6)](./index.mdx#eq-7-6), we express:

<Anchor id="eq-7-29" />
$$
\frac{Q_{N_2}(V_2,T)}{Q_N(V,T)} = e^{-\beta[A(N_2,V_2,T)-A(N,V,T)]} \approx e^{\beta(N_1\mu - V_1P)}, \tag{7.29}
$$

where the chemical potential $\mu$ and pressure $P$ are defined as derivatives of the reservoir’s Helmholtz free energy:

<Anchor id="eq-7-30" />
$$
\mu = \left.\frac{\partial A(N_2,V,T)}{\partial N_2}\right|_{N_2=N}, \tag{7.30}
$$

<Anchor id="eq-7-31" />
$$
P = -\left.\frac{\partial A(N,V_2,T)}{\partial V_2}\right|_{V_2=V}. \tag{7.31}
$$

Introducing the *fugacity*:

<Anchor id="eq-7-32" />
$$
z = e^{\beta\mu}, \tag{7.32}
$$

and substituting Eqs. [(7.29)](#eq-7-29) and [(7.32)](#eq-7-32) into Eq. [(7.27)](#eq-7-27), we obtain the probability distribution for the grand canonical ensemble:

<Anchor id="eq-7-33" />
$$
\rho(q,p,N) = z^N e^{-\beta PV-\beta\mathscr{H}(q,p)}, \tag{7.33}
$$

where the subscript “1” has been omitted for clarity. The influence of the reservoir is captured by the intensive parameters temperature $T$ and chemical potential $\mu$. The pressure $P$ is then determined by the equation of state.[^1] This equation of state, which involves the four thermodynamic quantities $(z,P,T,V)$, is determined by the normalization of the density function, Eq. [(7.33)](#eq-7-33). Consequently, for a general thermodynamic system, only three of these quantities are independent, and one of them must be the extensive variable $V$. Since the extensive properties of the reservoir are no longer relevant, we may take its thermodynamic limit, allowing the particle number of the subsystem to range from 0 to $\infty$.

To derive all thermodynamic functions, we define the *grand partition function*:

<Anchor id="eq-7-34" />
$$
\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T) = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty} z^N Q_N(V,T) = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^N}{N!h^{3N}}\int_V d^{3N}q\,d^{3N}p\,e^{-\beta\mathscr{H}(q,p)}, \tag{7.34}
$$

which depends on $z$ (or $\mu$), $V$, and $T$. Integrating Eq. [(7.33)](#eq-7-33) over phase space and summing over $N$ yields:

$$
1 = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\int_V\frac{d^{3N}q\,d^{3N}p}{N!h^{3N}}\,\rho(q,p,N) = e^{-\beta PV}\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T).
$$

Thus, the equation of state is given by:

<Anchor id="eq-7-35" />
$$
\frac{PV}{kT} = \ln\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T). \tag{7.35}
$$

The average particle number in the system is:

<Anchor id="eq-7-36" />
$$
\langle N\rangle = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty} N\frac{z^N Q_N(V,T)}{\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T)} = z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\ln\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T), \tag{7.36}
$$

which can also be expressed as the derivative w.r.t. $\mu$

<Anchor id="eq-7-37" />
$$
\langle N\rangle = kT\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\ln\mathcal{Q}(e^{\beta\mu},V,T). \tag{7.37}
$$

The internal energy is given by

<Anchor id="eq-7-38" />
$$
U = \langle\mathscr{H}\rangle = \frac{\sum_{N=0}^{\infty} z^N \int \frac{d^{3N}q\,d^{3N}p}{N!h^{3N}}\,\mathscr{H}\,e^{-\beta\mathscr{H}(q,p)}}{\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T)} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T). \tag{7.38}
$$

Using Eq. [(7.36)](#eq-7-36), we can replace the fugacity $z$ in Eq. [(7.38)](#eq-7-38) by the average particle number. Then, the internal energy $U$ becomes a function of $N$, $V$ and $T$. Other thermodynamic functions can be expressed as

$$
C_V = \left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_{N,V},
$$

$$
S = \int_0^T dT'\,\frac{C_V}{T'},\quad A = U - TS.
$$

Since the density function in Eq. [(7.33)](#eq-7-33) is normalized, the Shannon entropy is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\text{Shannon}} &= -k\sum_N\int\frac{d^{3N}q\,d^{3N}p}{N!h^{3N}}\,\rho(q,p,N)\ln\rho(q,p,N)\\
&= -k\sum_N\int\frac{d^{3N}q\,d^{3N}p}{N!h^{3N}}\,\rho(q,p,N)(N\ln z - \beta PV - \beta\mathscr{H})\\
&= \frac{U + PV - \mu\langle N\rangle}{T}.
\end{aligned}
$$

From the thermodynamic Euler relation, $U = TS - PV + \mu N$, it follows that this Shannon entropy is identical to the thermodynamic entropy.

[^1]: A gas has only two independent intensive parameters. If two intensive parameters, $T$ and $\mu$, are given, the remaining intensive parameter $P$ is fixed by the equation of state.
