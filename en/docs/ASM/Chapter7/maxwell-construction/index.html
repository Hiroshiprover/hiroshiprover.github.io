7.8 The Meaning of the Maxwell Construction

It has been established that the pressure PPP derived in the canonical ensemble coincides with that from the grand canonical ensemble if and only if ∂P/∂v≤0\partial P/\partial v \leq 0∂P/∂v≤0. In this section, we demonstrate that when ∂P/∂v>0\partial P/\partial v > 0∂P/∂v>0 for certain values of vvv —a situation relevant only in theoretical contexts or non-equilibrium state— the pressure in the grand canonical ensemble is obtained from the that in the canonical ensemble via the Maxwell construction.

Canonical ensemble​

Let the pressure calculated in the canonical ensemble be denoted by Pcan(v)P_{\text{can}}(v)Pcan​(v). At a special temperature, it may exhibit a positive derivative over a finite range, as shown qualitatively in Fig. 7.7. Suppose the partition function can be written as

QN(V)=eVF(v),(7.85)Q_N(V) = e^{VF(v)}, \tag{7.85}QN​(V)=eVF(v),(7.85)

where F(v)F(v)F(v) is another notation for f(v)f(v)f(v) defined in Eq. (7.66). In what follows, we also introduce the notation Φ(v,z)\Phi(v,z)Φ(v,z) in place of ϕ(v,z)\phi(v,z)ϕ(v,z) defined in Eq. (7.80). The purpose of these new notations is to distinguish the scenarios involving unstable states in the present section from those without such states in the preceding discussion. Almost all the results obtained in previous sections regarding f(v)f(v)f(v) and ϕ(v,z)\phi(v,z)ϕ(v,z) can be readily carried over to this section by replacing them with the newly defined notations. If such a direct translation is not possible, we shall point it out.

Note that both F(v)F(v)F(v) and Φ(v,z)\Phi(v,z)Φ(v,z) pertain to systems with a fixed specific volume vvv. Once the system volume VVV is fixed, such a system corresponds to a particular canonical ensemble with particle number N=V/vN = V/vN=V/v. Consequently, every state represented in the PcanP_{\text{can}}Pcan​–vvv diagram of Fig. 7.7 can be characterized by a suitable canonical ensemble.

Grand canonical ensemble​

Let us now examine the same scenario presented in Fig. 7.7, utilizing the grand canonical ensemble.

Note that the derivation of Eq. (7.77) is independent of the sign of ∂P/∂v\partial P/\partial v∂P/∂v. By the same reasoning, the grand partition function in the present case is also dominated by the largest of its summands,

lim⁡V→∞1Vln⁡Q(z,V)=max⁡[Φ(v,z)]≡Φ(vˉ,z).(7.90)\lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{1}{V}\ln\mathcal{Q}(z,V) = \max[\Phi(v,z)] \equiv \Phi(\bar{v},z). \tag{7.90}V→∞lim​V1​lnQ(z,V)=max[Φ(v,z)]≡Φ(vˉ,z).(7.90)

From Eqs. (7.86)–(7.88) and Fig. 7.7, we see that both Φ\PhiΦ and ∂Φ/∂v\partial\Phi/\partial v∂Φ/∂v are differentiable. The value of vˉ\bar{v}vˉ is therefore determined by the following conditions:

(∂Φ∂v)v=vˉ=0(∂2Φ∂v2)v=vˉ≤0(7.91)\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial v}\right)_{v=\bar{v}} &= 0\\ \left(\frac{\partial^2\Phi}{\partial v^2}\right)_{v=\bar{v}} &\leq 0 \end{aligned} \tag{7.91}(∂v∂Φ​)v=vˉ​(∂v2∂2Φ​)v=vˉ​​=0≤0​(7.91)

together with the following additional rule: if there are more than one value of vˉ\bar{v}vˉ, we must take the one that gives the largest Φ(vˉ,z)\Phi(\bar{v}, z)Φ(vˉ,z). These conditions determine the value of vˉ\bar{v}vˉ as a function of zzz, or vice versa. Then, the pressure in the grand canonical ensemble, denoted as Pgr(vˉ)P_{\text{gr}}(\bar{v})Pgr​(vˉ), is given by

βPgr(vˉ)=lim⁡V→∞1Vln⁡Q(z,V)=Φ(vˉ,z).(7.92)\beta P_{\text{gr}}(\bar{v}) = \lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{1}{V}\ln\mathcal{Q}(z,V) = \Phi(\bar{v},z). \tag{7.92}βPgr​(vˉ)=V→∞lim​V1​lnQ(z,V)=Φ(vˉ,z).(7.92)

By comparing Eq. (7.94) and Eq. (7.92), we then conclude that

Pgr(vˉ)=Pcan(vˉ).(7.95)P_{\text{gr}}(\bar{v}) = P_{\text{can}}(\bar{v}). \tag{7.95}Pgr​(vˉ)=Pcan​(vˉ).(7.95)

At first sight, the story seems to end with the conclusion that the two ensembles are equivalent even for ∂P/∂v>0\partial P/\partial v > 0∂P/∂v>0. However, the story is not over yet: the situations in the two ensembles are, in fact, different. The key point lies in the domain of vˉ\bar{v}vˉ. In the canonical ensemble, there is no further restriction on the specific volume vvv, whereas in the grand canonical ensemble vˉ\bar{v}vˉ must satisfy the condition in Eq. (7.91). Therefore, the domains of vˉ\bar{v}vˉ in the two ensembles may differ.

Maxwell Construction in Grand Canonical Ensemble​

First, vˉ\bar{v}vˉ cannot lie in the interval a≤v≤ba \leq v \leq ba≤v≤b. If it did, then, according to Eq. (7.89), ∂Φ/∂v=0\partial\Phi/\partial v = 0∂Φ/∂v=0 would imply ∂2Φ/∂v2>0\partial^2\Phi/\partial v^2 > 0∂2Φ/∂v2>0, in contradiction with Eq. (7.91). Outside this interval, on the other hand, the condition ∂Φ/∂v=0\partial\Phi/\partial v = 0∂Φ/∂v=0, which then also implies ∂2Φ/∂v2≤0\partial^2\Phi/\partial v^2 \leq 0∂2Φ/∂v2≤0, is by itself sufficient to determine vˉ\bar{v}vˉ. Using Eqs. (7.86) and (7.94), we obtain

βPcan(vˉ)=F(vˉ)+1vˉln⁡z⇒kTvˉF(vˉ)−vˉPcan(vˉ)=−kTln⁡z⇒∫v0vˉdv′ Pcan(v′)−vˉPcan(vˉ)=−kTln⁡z.(7.96)\begin{aligned} &\beta P_{\text{can}}(\bar{v}) = F(\bar{v}) + \frac{1}{\bar{v}}\ln z \Rightarrow kT\bar{v}F(\bar{v}) - \bar{v}P_{\text{can}}(\bar{v}) = -kT\ln z\\ &\Rightarrow \int_{v_0}^{\bar{v}}dv'\,P_{\text{can}}(v') - \bar{v}P_{\text{can}}(\bar{v}) = -kT\ln z. \end{aligned} \tag{7.96}​βPcan​(vˉ)=F(vˉ)+vˉ1​lnz⇒kTvˉF(vˉ)−vˉPcan​(vˉ)=−kTlnz⇒∫v0​vˉ​dv′Pcan​(v′)−vˉPcan​(vˉ)=−kTlnz.​(7.96)

This equation determines an implicit relation between vˉ\bar{v}vˉ and zzz. Differentiating both sides of Eq. (7.96) with respect to vˉ\bar{v}vˉ, we find

kT∂ln⁡z∂vˉ=vˉ∂Pcan∂vˉ.(7.97)kT\frac{\partial\ln z}{\partial\bar{v}} = \bar{v}\frac{\partial P_{\text{can}}}{\partial\bar{v}}. \tag{7.97}kT∂vˉ∂lnz​=vˉ∂vˉ∂Pcan​​.(7.97)

Since vˉ>0\bar{v} > 0vˉ>0, Eq. (7.97) suggests that the ln⁡z\ln zlnz–vˉ\bar{v}vˉ curve is similar in shape to the PcanP_{\text{can}}Pcan​–vvv curve. Both exhibit a minimum at aaa and a maximum at bbb. However, they differ in one key respect: in the grand canonical ensemble, vˉ\bar{v}vˉ cannot take values in the interval a<vˉ<ba < \bar{v} < ba<vˉ<b. Consequently, the plot of zzz versus vˉ\bar{v}vˉ in Fig. 7.8 shows a gap along the vˉ\bar{v}vˉ axis between aaa and bbb.

In addition, for a given value of zzz, Eq. (7.96) may have two roots for vˉ\bar{v}vˉ. For example, as shown in Fig. (7.8), the roots are AAA and BBB for z=z′z = z'z=z′, and B′B'B′ and A′A'A′ for z=z′′z = z''z=z′′. There exists a special value of zzz, denoted by z0z_0z0​, for which the two roots v1v_1v1​ and v2v_2v2​ satisfy the following two conditions:

∫v0v1,2dv′ βPcan(v′)−v1,2βPcan(v1,2)=−ln⁡z0,(7.98a)\int_{v_0}^{v_{1,2}}dv'\,\beta P_{\text{can}}(v') - v_{1,2}\beta P_{\text{can}}(v_{1,2}) = -\ln z_0, \tag{7.98a}∫v0​v1,2​​dv′βPcan​(v′)−v1,2​βPcan​(v1,2​)=−lnz0​,(7.98a) Φ(v1,z0)=Φ(v2,z0).(7.98b)\Phi(v_1,z_0) = \Phi(v_2,z_0). \tag{7.98b}Φ(v1​,z0​)=Φ(v2​,z0​).(7.98b)

Together with Eq. (7.88), Φ(v,z)=1v[ln⁡z+∫v0vdv′ βPcan(v′)]\Phi(v,z) = \frac{1}{v}[\ln z + \int_{v_0}^{v}dv'\,\beta P_{\text{can}}(v')]Φ(v,z)=v1​[lnz+∫v0​v​dv′βPcan​(v′)], these two conditions gives rise to

Φ(v1,z0)=1v1[ln⁡z0+∫v0v1dv′ βPcan(v′)]=βPcan(v1)Φ(v2,z0)=1v2[ln⁡z0+∫v0v2dv′ βPcan(v′)]=βPcan(v2)⇒Pcan(v1)=Pcan(v2)≡P0.\begin{aligned} \Phi(v_1,z_0) &= \frac{1}{v_1}\left[\ln z_0 + \int_{v_0}^{v_1}dv'\,\beta P_{\text{can}}(v')\right] = \beta P_{\text{can}}(v_1)\\ \Phi(v_2,z_0) &= \frac{1}{v_2}\left[\ln z_0 + \int_{v_0}^{v_2}dv'\,\beta P_{\text{can}}(v')\right] = \beta P_{\text{can}}(v_2)\\ &\Rightarrow P_{\text{can}}(v_1) = P_{\text{can}}(v_2) \equiv P_0. \end{aligned}Φ(v1​,z0​)Φ(v2​,z0​)​=v1​1​[lnz0​+∫v0​v1​​dv′βPcan​(v′)]=βPcan​(v1​)=v2​1​[lnz0​+∫v0​v2​​dv′βPcan​(v′)]=βPcan​(v2​)⇒Pcan​(v1​)=Pcan​(v2​)≡P0​.​

This means that at this special value z0z_0z0​, two specific volumes correspond to the same zzz, and the pressures at these two volumes are equal. We denote this common pressure by P0P_0P0​. However, it should be noted that for z≠z0z \neq z_0z=z0​, two specific volumes may also correspond to the same zzz, but the pressures at these two volumes are generally not equal.

Taking the difference between the two equations in Eq. (7.98a), corresponding respectively to v1v_1v1​ and v2v_2v2​, we obtain the following expression:

∫v1v2dv′ Pcan(v′)−(v2−v1)Pcan(v1)=0.(7.99)\int_{v_1}^{v_2}dv'\,P_{\text{can}}(v') - (v_2-v_1)P_{\text{can}}(v_1) = 0. \tag{7.99}∫v1​v2​​dv′Pcan​(v′)−(v2​−v1​)Pcan​(v1​)=0.(7.99)

This equation leads to the well-known Maxwell construction in the PcanP_{\text{can}}Pcan​–vvv diagram shown in Fig. 7.7.

Let us now return to the roots of Eq. (7.96) for vˉ\bar{v}vˉ at a general zzz, which are also shown graphically in Fig. 7.8. Outside the interval v1≤v≤v2v_1 \leq v \leq v_2v1​≤v≤v2​, the zzz–vvv curve is drawn as a solid line. Inside this interval but outside a≤v≤ba \leq v \leq ba≤v≤b, it is drawn as a dashed line. Next, we shall demonstrate that the dashed line must be discarded in the grand canonical ensemble.

Consider two points, AAA and BBB, in Fig. 7.8. They correspond to the same value of zzz, denoted by z′z'z′, and their specific volumes are denoted by vAv_AvA​ and vBv_BvB​, respectively. Since both are solutions of Eq. (7.96), the function Φ(v,z′)\Phi(v,z')Φ(v,z′) has a local maximum at each of v=vAv = v_Av=vA​ and v=vBv = v_Bv=vB​. We then have

Pcan(vB)>Pcan(vA),andΦ(vB,z′)>Φ(vA,z′).(7.101)P_{\text{can}}(v_B) > P_{\text{can}}(v_A),\quad\text{and}\quad \Phi(v_B,z') > \Phi(v_A,z'). \tag{7.101}Pcan​(vB​)>Pcan​(vA​),andΦ(vB​,z′)>Φ(vA​,z′).(7.101)

The pressure Pgr(vˉ)P_{\text{gr}}(\bar{v})Pgr​(vˉ) is plotted as solid lines in Fig. 7.9. We can, however, fill in a horizontal line at P0P_0P0​ by the usual arguments, namely, that since the systems at v1v_1v1​ and v2v_2v2​ have the same temperature, pressure, and chemical potential, a system at v1v_1v1​ can coexist with a system at v2v_2v2​ with any relative amount of each present.

Final remark: It is an experimental fact that ∂P/∂v≤0\partial P/\partial v \leq 0∂P/∂v≤0. It could not be otherwise: the system would then be in a highly unstable situation, in which reducing the pressure applied to it would cause it to shrink. The quantity PcanP_{\text{can}}Pcan​ is obtained from a (generally approximate) calculation and may or may not possess this desirable property. However, the corresponding pressure in the grand canonical ensemble always satisfies the stability condition, because the ensemble explicitly includes all possible density fluctuations of the system. Note that although the intervals v1<v<av_1 < v < av1​<v<a and b<v<v2b < v < v_2b<v<v2​ are excluded from the grand canonical ensemble, they also satisfy ∂P/∂v≤0\partial P/\partial v \leq 0∂P/∂v≤0 and are accessible as metastable states, corresponding respectively to a superheated liquid and a supercooled gas.
