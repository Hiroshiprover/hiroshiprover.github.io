Next, we shall cast the density fluctuation (more precisely, the second derivative ∂2P/∂μ2\partial^2 P/\partial\mu^2∂2P/∂μ2 appearing on the right-hand side) into a more familiar and physically transparent form. As an extensive quantity, the Helmholtz free energy A(N,V,T)A(N, V, T)A(N,V,T) satisfies the scaling relation:

A(λN,λV,T)=λA(N,V,T).A(\lambda N,\lambda V,T) = \lambda A(N,V,T).A(λN,λV,T)=λA(N,V,T).

Thus, we may express it as:

A(N,V)=Na(v),(7.40)A(N,V) = Na(v), \tag{7.40}A(N,V)=Na(v),(7.40)

where v=V/Nv = V/Nv=V/N is the volume per particle, and a(v)a(v)a(v) is the Helmholtz free energy per particle. For simplicity, we suppress the temperature TTT (a fixed external parameter) in the notation. The chemical potential μ\muμ and pressure PPP (both intensive) are then given by:

μ={∂[Na(v)]∂N}V=a(v)+N∂a∂v∂v∂N=a(v)−v∂a(v)∂v(7.41)\mu = \left\{\frac{\partial[Na(v)]}{\partial N}\right\}_V = a(v) + N\frac{\partial a}{\partial v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial N} = a(v) - v\frac{\partial a(v)}{\partial v} \tag{7.41}μ={∂N∂[Na(v)]​}V​=a(v)+N∂v∂a​∂N∂v​=a(v)−v∂v∂a(v)​(7.41) P=−{∂[Na(v)]∂V}N=−N∂a(v)∂v∂v∂V=−∂a(v)∂v.(7.42)P = -\left\{\frac{\partial[Na(v)]}{\partial V}\right\}_N = -N\frac{\partial a(v)}{\partial v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial V} = -\frac{\partial a(v)}{\partial v}. \tag{7.42}P=−{∂V∂[Na(v)]​}N​=−N∂v∂a(v)​∂V∂v​=−∂v∂a(v)​.(7.42)

⟨N2⟩−⟨N⟩2=kTV∂2P∂μ2=kTV1v2κT=⟨N⟩kTκT/v.(7.44)\langle N^2\rangle - \langle N\rangle^2 = kTV\frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial\mu^2} = kTV\frac{1}{v^2}\kappa_T = \langle N\rangle kT\kappa_T/v. \tag{7.44}⟨N2⟩−⟨N⟩2=kTV∂μ2∂2P​=kTVv21​κT​=⟨N⟩kTκT​/v.(7.44)

For most physical systems, the isothermal compressibility κT\kappa_TκT​ is positive and finite. In such cases, the relative fluctuation in the particle density is negligible. This is no longer true when κT\kappa_TκT​ diverges. Such a divergence can occur at a first-order phase transition, where (∂P/∂v)T=0(\partial P/\partial v)_T = 0(∂P/∂v)T​=0, and also at the critical point of a continuous phase transition.

Eq. (7.44) is analogous to Eq. (7.18) and provides another example of the fluctuation-dissipation theorem, which states that the fluctuations of a physical quantity are generally related to a corresponding generalized susceptibility. In Eq. (7.18), the energy fluctuation is related to the heat capacity at constant volume, CVC_VCV​; in Eq. (7.44), the density fluctuation is related to the isothermal compressibility, κT\kappa_TκT​.

The probability for a system to contain NNN particles in the grand canonical ensemble is proportional to:

W(N)=zNQN(V,T)=eβ[μN−A(N,V,T)],(7.45)W(N) = z^N Q_N(V,T) = e^{\beta[\mu N - A(N,V,T)]}, \tag{7.45}W(N)=zNQN​(V,T)=eβ[μN−A(N,V,T)],(7.45)

where A(N,V,T)A(N,V,T)A(N,V,T) is the Helmholtz free energy for the canonical ensemble with NNN particles. When density fluctuations are negligible (away from phase transitions), W(N)W(N)W(N) is sharply peaked at N=⟨N⟩N = \langle N\rangleN=⟨N⟩, with a width of order ⟨N⟩\sqrt{\langle N\rangle}⟨N⟩​. Thus, the most probable particle number is simply the mean particle number, Nˉ=⟨N⟩\bar{N} = \langle N\rangleNˉ=⟨N⟩. Furthermore,

Q(z,V,T)=∑NW(N)≈W(Nˉ).\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T) = \sum_N W(N) \approx W(\bar{N}).Q(z,V,T)=N∑​W(N)≈W(Nˉ).

From Eq. (7.45), the Helmholtz free energy is determined by its value at the most probable particle number, N=NˉN = \bar{N}N=Nˉ:

A(Nˉ,V,T)≈μNˉ−kTln⁡W(Nˉ)=kT[Nˉln⁡z−ln⁡Q(z,V,T)].(7.46)A(\bar{N},V,T) \approx \mu\bar{N} - kT\ln W(\bar{N}) = kT\left[\bar{N}\ln z - \ln\mathcal{Q}(z,V,T)\right]. \tag{7.46}A(Nˉ,V,T)≈μNˉ−kTlnW(Nˉ)=kT[Nˉlnz−lnQ(z,V,T)].(7.46)

This result agrees with the Euler relation A=U−TS=−PV+μNA = U - TS = -PV + \mu NA=U−TS=−PV+μN.

Remark: During a phase transition, the isothermal compressibility κT\kappa_TκT​ diverges, and density fluctuations become significant (as seen in critical opalescence). Nevertheless, Eq. (7.46) remains valid, as will be shown later in this chapter.
