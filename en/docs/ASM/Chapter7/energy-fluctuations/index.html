7.2 Energy Fluctuation in Canonical Ensemble

Although the canonical ensemble technically allows energy fluctuations, these fluctuations are negligible for macroscopic thermodynamic systems. As a result, the vast majority of systems in the ensemble reside within an extremely narrow energy range centered at the value determined by the temperature TTT. Hence, the canonical ensemble becomes equivalent to the microcanonical ensemble.

Consider the following expression

∫d3Nq d3NpN!h3N [U−H(q,p)] eβ[A(V,T)−H(q,p)]=0,(7.15)\int\frac{d^{3N}q\,d^{3N}p}{N!h^{3N}}\,[U-\mathscr{H}(q,p)]\,e^{\beta[A(V,T)-\mathscr{H}(q,p)]} = 0, \tag{7.15}∫N!h3Nd3Nqd3Np​[U−H(q,p)]eβ[A(V,T)−H(q,p)]=0,(7.15)

where U=⟨H⟩U = \langle\mathscr{H}\rangleU=⟨H⟩ is determined by TTT and VVV. Differentiating both sides of Eq. (7.15) with respect to β\betaβ, we find

∂U∂β+∫d3Nq d3NpN!h3N(U−H)(A−H+β∂A∂β)eβ(A−H)=0.(7.16)\frac{\partial U}{\partial\beta} + \int\frac{d^{3N}q\,d^{3N}p}{N!h^{3N}}(U-\mathscr{H})\left(A-\mathscr{H}+\beta\frac{\partial A}{\partial\beta}\right)e^{\beta(A-\mathscr{H})} = 0. \tag{7.16}∂β∂U​+∫N!h3Nd3Nqd3Np​(U−H)(A−H+β∂β∂A​)eβ(A−H)=0.(7.16)

Because β∂A∂β=−T∂A∂T=TS\beta\frac{\partial A}{\partial\beta} = -T\frac{\partial A}{\partial T} = TSβ∂β∂A​=−T∂T∂A​=TS, Eq. (7.16) can be written as

∂U∂β+⟨(U−H)2⟩=0.(7.17)\frac{\partial U}{\partial\beta} + \left\langle(U-\mathscr{H})^2\right\rangle = 0. \tag{7.17}∂β∂U​+⟨(U−H)2⟩=0.(7.17)

It gives rise to the mean square fluctuation of energy

⟨H2⟩−⟨H⟩2=⟨(U−H)2⟩=−∂U∂β=kT2(∂U∂T)V=kT2CV.(7.18)\langle\mathscr{H}^2\rangle - \langle\mathscr{H}\rangle^2 = \left\langle(U-\mathscr{H})^2\right\rangle = -\frac{\partial U}{\partial\beta} = kT^2\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_V = kT^2C_V. \tag{7.18}⟨H2⟩−⟨H⟩2=⟨(U−H)2⟩=−∂β∂U​=kT2(∂T∂U​)V​=kT2CV​.(7.18)

This identity connects the energy fluctuation with the heat capacity of the system. It is an example of the fluctuation-dissipation theorem. For a macroscopic system, both ⟨H⟩\langle\mathscr{H}\rangle⟨H⟩ and CVC_VCV​ are proportional to the particle number NNN. In the thermodynamic limit N→∞N \to \inftyN→∞, the relative energy fluctuation becomes negligible. Consequently, nearly all system replicas in the ensemble share the same energy value - the system’s internal energy UUU. This result is expected, as we have previously established that relative fluctuations of extensive quantities become negligible.

In what follows, we will present another heuristic derivation of the energy fluctuation, which shows a connection between the definitions of entropy in the microcanonical and canonical ensembles. We start with the partition function

∫d3Nq d3NpN!h3N e−βH(q,p)=∫0∞dE ω(E)e−βE=∫0∞dE e−βE+ln⁡ω(E)=∫0∞dE eβ[TS(E)−E],(7.19)\begin{aligned} \int\frac{d^{3N}q\,d^{3N}p}{N!h^{3N}}\,e^{-\beta\mathscr{H}(q,p)} &= \int_0^\infty dE\,\omega(E)e^{-\beta E} = \int_0^\infty dE\,e^{-\beta E+\ln\omega(E)}\\ &= \int_0^\infty dE\,e^{\beta[TS(E)-E]}, \end{aligned} \tag{7.19}∫N!h3Nd3Nqd3Np​e−βH(q,p)​=∫0∞​dEω(E)e−βE=∫0∞​dEe−βE+lnω(E)=∫0∞​dEeβ[TS(E)−E],​(7.19)

where ω(E)\omega(E)ω(E) is the density of state at fixed volume VVV and S(E)=kln⁡ω(E)S(E) = k\ln\omega(E)S(E)=klnω(E) gives the entropy for a microcanonical ensemble at fixed energy EEE. It is emphasized that the function S(E)S(E)S(E) is not yet the entropy of our canonical ensemble, but only a function of EEE in the present form.

In the light of the ideal gas example with NNN particles (Chapter 6), the density of states ω(E)\omega(E)ω(E) grows rapidly with energy, typically as ω(E)∝EN\omega(E) \propto E^Nω(E)∝EN, while the Boltzmann factor e−βEe^{-\beta E}e−βE decreases exponentially. Consequently, the integrand in Eq. (7.19) exhibits a sharp peak at the most probable value of energy EEE. The width ΔE\Delta EΔE of the peak represents the relative energy fluctuation, which become negligible for macroscopic systems in the thermodynamic limit. In fact, the maximum of the integrand occurs at E=EˉE = \bar{E}E=Eˉ, where Eˉ\bar{E}Eˉ satisfies the condition

T(∂S∂E)E=Eˉ=1,(7.20)T\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial E}\right)_{E=\bar{E}} = 1, \tag{7.20}T(∂E∂S​)E=Eˉ​=1,(7.20) (∂2S∂E2)E=Eˉ<0,(7.21)\left(\frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial E^2}\right)_{E=\bar{E}} < 0, \tag{7.21}(∂E2∂2S​)E=Eˉ​<0,(7.21)

Recall that in the framework of the microcanonical ensemble, the partial derivative ∂S∂E\frac{\partial S}{\partial E}∂E∂S​ yields the inverse of temperature which is assumed uniformly by all macroscopic subsystems of an isolated system with a given energy. In the current discussion of canonical ensemble, the temperature TTT is prescribed by the heat reservoir. The first condition Eq. (7.20) consequently determines the most probable energy value Eˉ\bar{E}Eˉ, which we identify as the internal energy UUU of the system. In any case, Eq. (7.20) can be interpreted as defining an implicit functional relationship between UUU and TTT at constant system volume.

(∂2S∂E2)E=Eˉ=(∂T−1∂E)E=Eˉ=−1T2(∂T∂E)E=Eˉ=−1T2CV.(7.22)\left(\frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial E^2}\right)_{E=\bar{E}} = \left(\frac{\partial T^{-1}}{\partial E}\right)_{E=\bar{E}} = -\frac{1}{T^2}\left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial E}\right)_{E=\bar{E}} = -\frac{1}{T^2C_V}. \tag{7.22}(∂E2∂2S​)E=Eˉ​=(∂E∂T−1​)E=Eˉ​=−T21​(∂E∂T​)E=Eˉ​=−T2CV​1​.(7.22)

Since the heat capacity is always positive for realistic physical systems, the second condition Eq. (7.21) is satisfied automatically.

This expansion shows that in the canonical ensemble the energy distribution is Gaussian, centered around the mean energy UUU with width

ΔE=2kT2CV.(7.24)\Delta E = \sqrt{2kT^2C_V}. \tag{7.24}ΔE=2kT2CV​​.(7.24)

Since the first two terms are extensive (scaling with NNN), the third term—which scales as ln⁡N\ln NlnN—becomes negligible in the thermodynamic limit. Neglecting this logarithmic contribution and comparing with Eq. (7.12), we find that the canonical entropy defined in Eq. (7.13) coincides with the microcanonical entropy evaluated at the internal energy UUU of the canonical ensemble:

S=kln⁡ω(U)=−(∂A∂T)V.S = k\ln\omega(U) = -\left(\frac{\partial A}{\partial T}\right)_V.S=klnω(U)=−(∂T∂A​)V​.

External and random quantities. For an isolated system, its total energy is a strictly conserved quantity that does not exhibit thermal fluctuations (note that the small Δ\DeltaΔ in the definition of the microcanonical ensemble represents an energy resolution width rather than thermal fluctuations). This energy is fixed and can only be adjusted through external means. We refer to such parameters as external quantities. When considering its subsystems, each subsystem’s energy may fluctuate around their mean values (the most probable values) while preserving the total energy conservation. We call such quantities as random variables. The temperature, being determined by the equilibrium condition (most probable state) among all subsystems, likewise constitutes a random variable. In contrast, a system in the canonical ensemble can be viewed as a macroscopic subsystem of an isolated system. Consequently, its internal energy behaves as a random variable, while the temperature remains fixed by the external heat reservoir (its fluctuation can be ignored), thereby serving as an external parameter.
